Rules and Guidelines

Ready, Spec, Ship Hackathon Rules
Everything you need to know to participate in the Ready, Spec, Ship Hackathon.

Everything you need to know to participate in the Ready, Spec, Ship Hackathon.

1. Host and Sponsor
The legal Host of the hackathon is Trivial Business Limited.

John Crickett acts as the representative of Trivial Business Limited.

The Official Sponsor is Kiro.

2. No Purchase Necessary
No purchase or payment is required to enter or win.

Making a purchase or payment will not improve a participant's chances of winning.

3. Important Dates
All dates and times are in Coordinated Universal Time, or UTC.

Submissions Open: August 8, 2026 at 00:00 UTC
Submission Deadline: August 23, 2026 at 23:59 UTC
Judging Period: August 24 to September 5, 2026
Winners Announced: By September 6, 2026 at 23:59 UTC
The period from August 8, 2026 at 00:00 UTC until August 23, 2026 at 23:59 UTC is referred to as the Competition Period.

4. Agreement to the Rules
By entering the hackathon, each participant agrees to be bound by:

These Rules and Guidelines
The separate Prizes page
The separate Judging Criteria page
The final decisions of the Host, Sponsor, and judges
If there is any inconsistency between these Rules and Guidelines and the Prizes or Judging Criteria pages, these Rules and Guidelines will control.

5. Eligibility
Age Requirement
Each participant must have reached the age of majority in the jurisdiction where they live at the time of entry.

Eligible Entrants
Submissions may be entered by:

An individual participant
A team of two or three participants
Organizations, companies, partnerships, charities, and other legal entities may not submit projects directly.

Geographic Eligibility
Participation is open worldwide except where prohibited by applicable law.

The hackathon is not open to:

Individuals residing in a country, state, province, territory, or region where United States, United Kingdom, European Union, or local law prohibits participation or receipt of a prize in the Hackathon (including, but not limited to, Brazil, Quebec, Russia, Crimea, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, or Syria).
Individuals or entities subject to applicable United States, United Kingdom, or European Union sanctions.
Any person whose participation or receipt of a prize would cause the Host or Sponsor to breach applicable sanctions, export controls, or other laws.
This includes restrictions administered under applicable United States, United Kingdom, and European Union sanctions regimes.

Ineligible Participants
Participation is open worldwide except where prohibited or restricted by applicable law.

The Hackathon is not open to:

Individuals residing in a country, state, province, territory, or region where participation in the Hackathon, or the receipt or payment of a prize, would be unlawful or would require a licence, registration, authorization, or regulatory approval that the Host or Sponsor has not obtained.
Residents of Brazil, Quebec, Russia, Crimea, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, or Syria.
Individuals subject to applicable United States, United Kingdom, or European Union sanctions.
Any person whose participation or receipt of a prize would cause the Host or Sponsor to breach applicable sanctions, export controls, payment restrictions, or other laws.
Employees of Trivial Business Limited, AWS, Kiro and their affiliates.
Eligibility will be determined using the sanctions laws and official sanctions lists applicable at the time of entry and prize distribution.

Team Eligibility
Every member of a team must satisfy all eligibility requirements.

If any team member is found to be ineligible, the entire team and its submission will be disqualified.

6. Teams
Team Representative
Each team must appoint one eligible member as its official representative.

The representative is authorized to:

Submit the project on behalf of the team
Receive hackathon communications
Provide required documentation
Receive any prize awarded to the team
By submitting a project, the representative confirms that all team members have authorized them to act on the team's behalf.

Team Membership
A participant may:

Belong to more than one team
Submit projects individually
Participate in both individual and team submissions
Team membership becomes final at the submission deadline.

No team members may be added, removed, or replaced after August 23, 2026 at 23:59 UTC.

Team Contributions
Each team submission must:

Identify every registered team member
Briefly describe each team member's contribution to the project
7. Multiple Submissions
Participants may submit more than one project, either individually or as part of different teams.

Each submission must be unique and substantially different from every other submission involving the same participant.

8. Financial or Preferential Support
A submission must not have been developed, or derived from a project developed, using prior financial or preferential support from the Host, Sponsor, or their affiliates where that support would create an actual or apparent conflict of interest.

Relevant support may include:

Funding or investment
Paid development contracts
Commercial licensing arrangements
Commissioned work
Preferential technical assistance
Non-public access, resources, or benefits unavailable to other participants
Other support that could provide an unfair competitive advantage
Participants must disclose any relevant prior relationship or support when requested.

The Host may disqualify a participant, team, or submission where awarding a prize would create an actual or apparent conflict of interest.

9. Original Work
All submitted projects must be original work created during the Competition Period.

Pre-existing projects and codebases are prohibited.

Participants may not submit:

A project started before the Competition Period
A pre-existing codebase with minor modifications
A previously submitted or commercially released project
Work created by someone outside the registered team
All substantive development, design, documentation, and submission work must be completed by the individual participant or registered team members.

Paid contractors and outside contributors may not create, develop, design, or otherwise contribute to a submission.

Open-Source Tools
Participants may use:

Open-source libraries
Frameworks
Development tools
Generic starter templates
Participants must comply with all applicable licences and attribution requirements.

Kiro-Generated Code
AI-generated code created through Kiro is permitted, encouraged, and expected.

Participants remain responsible for reviewing, testing, securing, and understanding all code included in their submission.

Human Direction and Responsibility
Use of Kiro and AI-generated code is encouraged and expected.

However, each submission must demonstrate meaningful human direction, judgement, creativity, and effort from the individual participant or registered team.

Participants remain responsible for:

The project concept and product decisions
Reviewing and understanding generated code
Testing the project
Correcting errors
Protecting security and privacy
Confirming compliance with licences and third-party rights
Ensuring that the submission satisfies these Rules and Guidelines
A project that consists primarily of unreviewed or minimally directed AI-generated output may receive reduced judging scores or may be disqualified where meaningful participant contribution cannot be demonstrated.

10. Required Use of Kiro
Every submitted project must be built using Kiro.

Participants must clearly demonstrate meaningful use of Kiro in:

The project README
The demo video
A submission may be disqualified if its use of Kiro is superficial, incidental, or cannot be adequately demonstrated.

11. Third-Party APIs, Services, and Materials
Participants may use third-party APIs, SDKs, platforms, services, datasets, and other tools.

Participants must:

Be authorized to use each third-party resource
Comply with all applicable terms and licences
Follow usage restrictions
Provide required attribution
Obtain all necessary rights, consents, and permissions
The README must document:

Any costs or paid requirements
Rate limits or usage restrictions
Required setup and configuration
Test credentials or testing instructions
Third-party libraries, frameworks, APIs, datasets, and assets used
12. Intellectual Property and Third-Party Rights
Each participant represents and confirms that their submission:

Is their original work
Is owned by the participant or registered team
Does not infringe another person's intellectual property rights
Does not violate privacy, publicity, contractual, or other legal rights
Participants must have all necessary rights, licences, consents, and permissions for any:

Code
APIs
Datasets
Images
Audio
Video
Fonts
Trademarks
Personal information
Other third-party materials
13. Personal, Confidential, and Sensitive Data
Submissions must not include or use personal, confidential, proprietary, or sensitive data unless the participant has all necessary consent, permissions, and legal authorization.

Participants must not expose:

Real passwords
API keys
Access tokens
Private encryption keys
Production credentials
Confidential business information
Other secrets
14. Prohibited Content and Software
Submissions must not contain:

Malware
Viruses
Trojan horses
Destructive code
Credential-stealing functionality
Spyware
Hidden cryptocurrency mining
Unauthorized tracking
Other harmful or malicious software
Submissions must comply with all applicable laws and regulations.

15. Submission Requirements
Each submission must include all of the following:

A short written project description
A public source-code repository
The project's .kiro directory at the repository root
A complete README
A working demo, deployed application, or test build
A demo video
Testing instructions
Any required test credentials
Identification of all team members and their contributions, where applicable
There are no separate submission categories.

Project Description
The written project description must explain:

The problem being addressed
The proposed solution
The project's key features
Functionality and Accuracy of the Submission
The submitted project must be capable of being successfully installed or accessed and must run consistently on the platform for which it is intended.

The project must function substantially as:

Shown in the demo video
Described in the written project description
Explained in the README
Presented in any other submission materials
Participants must not misrepresent incomplete, simulated, hard-coded, or non-functional features as fully working functionality.

Minor defects may affect judging scores. Material differences between the submitted project and its description or demonstration may result in disqualification.

Public Repository
Each submission must include a public source-code repository that judges can access throughout the judging period.

The repository must contain everything reasonably necessary for judges to install, run, understand, and evaluate the project. This includes, where applicable:

All source code
Required assets
Dependency and package manifests
Configuration examples
Database schemas or migration files
Build and deployment instructions
Setup and installation instructions
Testing instructions
Any other files required for the project to function as submitted
Participants must not omit essential code or materials required to reproduce the submitted project.

Secrets, credentials, private keys, and other sensitive information must not be committed to the repository. Where configuration values are required, participants should provide safe examples and clear setup instructions.

An open-source licence is not required.

Kiro Project Files
Each submission repository must include the project's .kiro directory at the repository root.

Participants must not exclude the .kiro directory, or any of its contents, through .gitignore or similar repository settings.

The directory must contain the Kiro materials used during development, where applicable, including:

Specs
Hooks
Steering files
Configuration
Other materials demonstrating how Kiro was used
Judges must be able to inspect these materials when evaluating the submission.

Failure to include the .kiro directory, or intentionally concealing relevant Kiro materials, may result in disqualification.

README
Each submission must include a README containing:

Clear setup instructions
Clear usage instructions
An explanation of how Kiro was used
Any required configuration
API or service costs
Rate limits and usage restrictions
Testing instructions
Test credentials, where required
Attribution for third-party libraries, frameworks, APIs, datasets, and assets
16. Demo Video
Each submission must include a demo video that:

Shows the project working
Explains the problem and solution
Demonstrates the project's key features
Explains how Kiro was used
Is no longer than three minutes
Is accessible to judges without requesting permission
Judges are not required to watch beyond the three-minute limit.

The video must be hosted on a publicly accessible platform, such as:

YouTube
Vimeo
Loom
A similar publicly accessible video platform
The video may be unlisted, provided that anyone with the link can view it without requesting access or signing into a restricted account.

Participants must have permission to use all music, trademarks, video clips, images, and other material included in the video.

17. Testing Access
Each submission must include a working demo, deployed application, or test build that judges can access free of charge throughout the judging period.

Where authentication is required, participants must provide:

Working test credentials
Clear login instructions
Any necessary setup or configuration instructions
Participants must ensure that judges can reasonably evaluate the project without making a payment.

18. Language Requirements
All submission materials must be in English.

If any material is not in English, the participant must provide an English translation of:

The demo video
The project description
The README
Testing instructions
All other submitted materials
19. Submission Receipt
Only submissions successfully received by the submission platform before the deadline will qualify.

Proof of an attempted upload, email, or other submission effort will not count as proof of receipt.

Participants are responsible for:

Submitting their projects on time
Confirming that the submission was successfully received
Keeping backup copies of all submission materials
20. Technical Resubmission Requests
If a technical failure prevents a submission from being successfully received, the participant may request permission to resubmit.

The request must be made within 24 hours of the submission deadline, no later than:

August 24, 2026 at 23:59 UTC

The Host and Sponsor may approve or deny the request at their sole discretion.

Approval is not guaranteed.

Any approved resubmission must remain substantively the same as the original attempted submission.

21. Submission Modifications
After the submission deadline, submissions are locked.

Participants may not update:

Source code
Documentation
The README
The demo video
Submission links
Testing credentials
The working application
Team membership
Other substantive submission materials
Until the judging period ends, the Host and Sponsor may permit limited changes solely to:

Add, remove, or replace material that may infringe a third party's rights
Remove personally identifiable information
Remove or replace material that is inappropriate
Correct an issue specifically authorized by the Host or Sponsor
Any approved modification must leave the submission substantively unchanged.

This limited modification permission expires when the judging period ends on September 5, 2026.

22. Judging Process
Submissions will be evaluated in two rounds, followed by a tie-break process where required.

Judges may evaluate projects using:

The written project description
The README
The source-code repository
The demo video
The working application or test build
Other submitted materials
Judges are not required to test every working application during every judging round.

Projects that advance may receive more detailed technical testing during the second round.

Round One: Eligibility and Viability Screening
Round One is a pass-or-fail review.

Each submission must:

Meet all eligibility requirements
Include all required submission materials
Be functional enough to demonstrate its core purpose
Use Kiro in a meaningful way
Comply with these Rules and Guidelines
Only submissions that pass Round One will advance to Round Two.

Round Two: Scored Evaluation
Submissions that pass Round One will be scored using the 100-point judging rubric.

Category	Criterion	Points
Application Quality	Functionality & Completeness	15
Real-World Value	15
Code Quality	10
Category Total	40
Kiro Usage	Effective Use of Features	10
Workflows	10
Category Total	20
Documentation	Completeness	10
Clarity	5
Process Detail	5
Category Total	20
Innovation and Potential	Creativity	8
Originality	7
Category Total	15
Presentation	Demo Video	5
Category Total	5
Total	100
The separate Judging Criteria page forms part of these hackathon terms.

23. Tie-Breaking
If two or more submissions receive the same total score, ties will be resolved by comparing category scores in this order:

Application Quality
Kiro Usage
Documentation
Innovation and Potential
Presentation
If a tie remains after all category scores have been compared, the judging panel will vote to determine the higher-ranked submission.

24. Judges' Decisions
All judging decisions are final and binding, subject to applicable law.

This includes decisions concerning:

Eligibility
Compliance
Scoring
Ranking
Tie-breaking
Disqualification
Prize selection
25. Code of Conduct
Participants must act professionally and respectfully.

The following conduct is prohibited:

Plagiarism
Submission of another person's work
Harassment
Threatening or abusive conduct
Discrimination
Fraud or deception
Interference with other participants
Interference with the submission or judging process
Violation of applicable laws or regulations
26. Disqualification and Enforcement
The Host and Sponsor may disqualify a participant, team, or submission for:

Violating eligibility requirements
Plagiarism
Intellectual property infringement
Providing false or misleading information
Submitting incomplete or non-functional work
Failing to provide required documentation
Failing to demonstrate meaningful use of Kiro
Submitting after the deadline
Violating the Code of Conduct
Violating applicable laws
Fraud, deception, or misrepresentation
Tampering with the submission or judging process
Attempting to influence or interfere with judges
Compromising the fairness, security, or integrity of the hackathon
Any other material violation of these Rules and Guidelines
27. Verification
The Host and Sponsor may request reasonable evidence to verify:

Participant eligibility
Identity
Authorship
Ownership of the submission
Team membership
Each team member's contribution
Compliance with these Rules and Guidelines
Rights and permissions for third-party materials
The accuracy of information provided
Failure to provide requested evidence within a reasonable period may result in disqualification.

28. Intellectual Property Ownership
Participants retain ownership of their submissions.

Nothing in these Rules transfers ownership of a submission to the Host or Sponsor.

29. Promotional Licence
By submitting a project, each participant grants the Host and Sponsor a:

Worldwide
Non-exclusive
Royalty-free
licence for two years from the submission date.

The licence permits the Host and Sponsor to:

Showcase the project
Reproduce submission materials
Display submission materials
Promote the project and hackathon
Create winner announcements
Create blog posts or case studies
Share the project on websites and social media
Include the project in hackathon-related promotional materials
The Host and Sponsor may edit, crop, excerpt, or adapt submission materials for these purposes, provided that they do not materially misrepresent the project.

Participants also authorize the Host and Sponsor to use their:

Names
Likenesses
Voices
Biographies
Team names
for the same purposes and two-year period.

The Host and Sponsor will credit the participant or team where reasonably practical.

30. Prizes
Prize names, values, quantities, and eligibility requirements will be listed on a separate Prizes page.

The Prizes page forms part of these hackathon terms.

Multiple Prize Eligibility
Each submission may win no more than one prize.

A participant may receive prizes through multiple winning submissions, including submissions entered:

Individually
As part of different teams
Prize Availability
The Host and Sponsor may decline to award a prize if no eligible submission meets the required standard.

Prize Substitution
If a prize becomes unavailable, the Host and Sponsor may substitute it with a prize of equal or greater value.

Non-Transferability
Prizes are non-transferable.

A prize may only be awarded to the winning individual or to the designated representative of a winning team.

This does not prevent a team representative from distributing a team prize among registered team members.

31. Winner Documentation and Prize Distribution
Winners may be announced before completing identity, eligibility, tax, or prize documentation.

Prizes will be distributed within 60 days of the winner announcement, provided the winner has supplied all required documentation.

Prizes will only be distributed to winners who provide all required:

Identity documentation
Eligibility documentation
Tax documentation
Prize acceptance documentation
Payment information
If a winner fails to provide the required documentation, the winner will forfeit the prize.

The Host and Sponsor may then offer the prize to the next highest-ranked eligible submission.

32. Team Prize Distribution
For a winning team, the prize will be paid to the team's designated representative.

The representative may divide the prize among registered team members at their discretion.

The Host and Sponsor are not responsible for:

Deciding how a team prize should be divided
Distributing separate payments to team members
Resolving disputes between team members
Enforcing private agreements between team members
33. Fees and Taxes
Winners, including all members of a winning team, are responsible for any fees associated with receiving or using a prize.

This includes:

Bank transfer fees
Wiring fees
Currency-conversion fees
Payment-processing fees
Other related charges
Winners are responsible for reporting and paying all applicable taxes in their jurisdiction, including any:

Federal taxes
State taxes
Provincial taxes
Territorial taxes
Local taxes
Other applicable taxes
Winners may be required to provide information and complete tax or other forms needed for:

Prize payment
Tax withholding
Tax reporting
Legal compliance
34. Privacy
Personal information will be used only for:

Hackathon administration
Eligibility verification
Judging
Communications
Prize distribution
Tax and payment processing
Winner announcements
Personal information may be shared with:

Judges
Service providers
Payment processors
Tax-processing partners
Other parties where reasonably necessary for these purposes
Personal information will be retained only for as long as necessary to:

Administer the hackathon
Distribute prizes
Meet legal obligations
Meet tax and accounting obligations
Maintain required records
Participants may request access to, correction of, or deletion of their personal information, subject to applicable legal and record-retention requirements.

Personal information will be handled in accordance with applicable data protection laws, including the GDPR and CCPA where applicable.

35. Technical Issues
The Host and Sponsor are not responsible for:

Technical failures
Service outages
Interrupted internet access
Data loss
Corrupted files
Failed uploads
Lost submissions
Misdirected submissions
Platform errors
Hardware or software failures
Problems outside their reasonable control
Participants are responsible for maintaining backup copies of all submission materials.

36. Changes to the Hackathon
The Host and Sponsor may cancel, suspend, postpone, or modify the hackathon, in whole or in part, where reasonably necessary because of:

Technical failures
Fraud
Security incidents
Legal requirements
Events outside their reasonable control
Other circumstances that compromise the fair or safe operation of the hackathon
The Host and Sponsor may adjust:

Dates
Submission procedures
Judging arrangements
Prizes
Other administrative requirements
Material changes will be communicated to participants.

37. Changes to the Rules
The Host and Sponsor may update these Rules and Guidelines where reasonably necessary.

Material changes will:

Be communicated to participants
Apply prospectively
Not unfairly disadvantage work already completed or submitted
38. Assumption of Risk
Participants accept responsibility for risks arising from:

Participation in the hackathon
Creation and submission of a project
Use of third-party services
Acceptance or use of any prize
This provision applies except where applicable law provides otherwise.

No compensation will be provided for participation beyond any prizes awarded.

39. Release of Liability
To the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, participants release the Host, Sponsor, judges, affiliates, service providers, and their respective officers, directors, employees, contractors, representatives, and agents from claims, losses, damages, liabilities, costs, or expenses arising from or connected with:

Participation in the hackathon
Creation or submission of a project
Evaluation or judging of a project
Promotion of a project
Acceptance, receipt, use, or misuse of a prize
Technical failures
Circumstances outside their reasonable control
This release does not apply where liability cannot legally be excluded or limited.

40. Indemnification
To the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, participants agree to indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the Host, Sponsor, judges, affiliates, service providers, and their respective officers, directors, employees, contractors, representatives, and agents from third-party claims, losses, damages, liabilities, costs, and reasonable legal fees arising from:

The participant's submission
The participant's conduct
A breach of these Rules and Guidelines
Intellectual property infringement
Violation of privacy or publicity rights
Violation of contractual or other third-party rights
Unlawful or unauthorized use of data, APIs, services, software, or other materials
41. Informal Dispute Resolution
Before starting court proceedings, the participant and the Host or Sponsor must first make a reasonable, good-faith effort to resolve the dispute informally.

The informal resolution period will last 30 days from the date written notice of the dispute is received.

Court proceedings may begin after that period if the dispute remains unresolved.

42. Governing Law and Jurisdiction
These Rules and Guidelines, and any dispute arising from or connected with the hackathon, will be governed by the laws of England and Wales.

The courts of England and Wales will have exclusive jurisdiction over any such dispute.

43. Severability
If any provision of these Rules and Guidelines is found to be invalid, illegal, or unenforceable, that provision will be limited or removed only to the extent necessary.

The remaining provisions will continue in full force and effect.

44. No Waiver
A failure or delay by the Host or Sponsor in enforcing any provision of these Rules and Guidelines will not waive that provision or prevent its later enforcement.
